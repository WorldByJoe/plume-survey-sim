# Plume - a mobile methane leak-survey simulator

A self-contained, browser-based simulation of an advanced mobile leak
detection (AMLD) survey. A survey vehicle drives a real street network while
natural-gas leaks release methane into a realistically variable wind; the car
records concentration and wind, accumulates evidence on a map, and ends each
episode with a verdict: which leaks the survey found, and which it missed.

**View it here: https://worldbyjoe.github.io/plume-survey-sim/**

It runs entirely in the browser - no server, no accounts, no data collection -
and loops forever: each episode draws a fresh neighbourhood, weather scenario,
and set of leaks.

## What is being simulated

- **Emission and transport** - each leak releases a stream of puffs that ride
  the wind present at the moment of release, so a plume is a record of the
  wind's recent history laid out in space: it snakes. Dispersion follows
  Gaussian-puff spreading with Pasquill-Gifford coefficients.
- **Wind** - a mean transport vector that wanders slowly (the between-pass
  shifts a mesoscale model cannot see) plus fast per-component
  Ornstein-Uhlenbeck turbulence. Speed gustiness and direction meander both
  emerge from one sigma with the correct speed dependence, and the direction
  spread saturates toward ~104 degrees (the projected-normal limit) as the
  wind dies. The relationships were validated against a 2024 field
  calibration study (9.2 million calibrated 10 Hz anemometer samples over
  420 survey hours) and the boundary-layer literature (Hanna 1983;
  Sagendorf & Dickson 1974; Cirillo & Poli 1992; Mahrt 2011; Anfossi et
  al. 2005). Citations sit beside the code in the page source.
- **Detection and inference** - each drive-by sample updates a log-odds
  evidence map: a detection adds evidence across the wedge of ground upwind
  of the car, and clean air is a real observation that subtracts evidence -
  if a strong source were upwind, something would have been seen. Leak
  hypotheses are scored against an absolute evidence floor, and a leak once
  found stays found, reported with its best miss distance.
- **Coverage** - pellets along the roadway are eaten as the wheels pass over
  them, showing at a glance which streets each pass has actually covered.

## Scenarios

Four weather/effort scenarios rotate: a brisk steady wind, a light meandering
wind (the hard case - the upwind arc refuses to close), the same streets
driven four times in neutral air, and a wind that shifts between passes.
Street networks are real neighbourhoods; pin one with `?hood=` (`norcross`,
`trussville`, `bowie`, `ftcollins`, `levittown`, `northend`, `chicago`,
`tulane`).

## Provenance

Generated by script from the live source of the author's development copy,
so this published version cannot drift out of step with the model as it
improves. The wind model is grounded in the author's field studies of
mobile leak-detection surveys and controlled methane releases.
